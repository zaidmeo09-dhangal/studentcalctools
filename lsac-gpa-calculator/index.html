THE BASICS

What Is LSAC (CAS) GPA?

When you apply to an ABA-approved law school, the Law School Admission Council (LSAC) does not simply forward your transcript. Through its Credential Assembly Service (CAS), LSAC recalculates your entire undergraduate academic record onto a single standardized scale, so that every applicant is compared using the same rules regardless of which university they attended or how that university calculates its own GPA.

This recalculated number, called your LSAC GPA or CAS GPA, is what every law school actually sees. It is the figure schools report to the American Bar Association for median GPA statistics, and admissions committees do not have access to a different number. Your transcript is also sent along with the report, but the standardized GPA is the headline figure.

This calculator estimates that number using LSAC's published grade scale and credit-weighted formula, so you can see roughly where you stand before you register for CAS.

2027 RULE CHANGE

2026-2027 vs 2027-2028: The Dual Enrollment Change

In mid-2026, LSAC announced that it would stop counting grades from college-level courses taken while a candidate was still in high school, commonly called dual enrollment courses, toward the LSAC GPA. The original effective date was July 28, 2026. After feedback that candidates in the 2026-2027 cycle had not been given enough notice, LSAC delayed the change.

Where things stand right now

For the 2026-2027 application cycle, dual enrollment grades are still included in the LSAC GPA exactly as they have been for years.

Starting with the 2027-2028 application cycle, LSAC will exclude dual enrollment grades from the GPA calculation for every applicant in that cycle and beyond, including reapplicants.

Admissions staff will still see dual enrollment coursework on the CAS report with an academic note, similar to how AP coursework is already reported. The change only affects whether those grades are folded into the numeric GPA. Use the cycle selector in the calculator above to compare your LSAC GPA under both versions of the policy.

Formula
LSAC GPA = (Sum of LSAC grade points × credit hours) ÷ total graded credit hours

If your school runs on a quarter system, LSAC converts quarter credit hours to semester credit hours by multiplying by 0.67. This calculator asks for credits in semester hours, so convert quarter credits before entering them.

WHAT COUNTS

What's Included and Excluded

The arithmetic behind the LSAC GPA is simple. What makes it different from your transcript GPA is which courses go into that arithmetic in the first place.

Included

  • Every undergraduate course before your first bachelor's degree
  • Both attempts of a repeated course
  • Community college and transfer credits
  • Study abroad grades on your domestic transcript
  • Dual enrollment grades, for the 2026-2027 cycle only
  • Punitive withdrawals (WF), counted as 0.00

Excluded

  • Coursework after your first bachelor's degree, including graduate work
  • Non-punitive withdrawals (W, WP)
  • Incomplete grades, if non-punitive
  • Pass/Fail passing grades (P, CR, S)
  • Remedial courses clearly marked as such
  • Dual enrollment grades, starting the 2027-2028 cycle

Academic forgiveness and grade replacement policies at your own school do not change any of this. If a grade and its credit hours still appear on your official transcript, LSAC includes it, even if your school no longer counts it toward your own GPA.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A Worked Example: The Retake

Suppose you failed a 4-credit course in your first year, then retook it the following year and earned an A-. Your school uses grade replacement, so your transcript GPA reflects only the retake.

First attempt: F, 4 credits → 0.00 × 4 = 0.00 grade points

Retake: A-, 4 credits → 3.67 × 4 = 14.68 grade points

Your school GPA only shows the A-. LSAC averages both attempts together: 14.68 total grade points across 8 total credit hours, for a 1.84 average from these two rows alone. Spread across a full four-year transcript, this single retake can pull your overall LSAC GPA down noticeably compared to what your diploma shows.

Enter both rows separately in the calculator above to see this effect on your own transcript.

COMMON MISTAKES

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming grade forgiveness applies. A forgiven or replaced grade still counts if it appears anywhere on your official transcript.
  • Entering A+ as 4.00. On the LSAC scale it is 4.33, and skipping this undercounts your real GPA.
  • Confusing WF with W. A WF counts as a failing 0.00. A plain W or WP has no effect on the GPA at all.
  • Leaving out community college or transfer credits. LSAC includes coursework from every institution you attended, not just your degree-granting school.
  • Including graduate coursework. Only work completed before your first bachelor's degree counts toward this GPA.
  • Picking the wrong application cycle. Dual enrollment treatment depends on whether you are applying in the 2026-2027 cycle or the 2027-2028 cycle onward.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is LSAC GPA and how is it different from my school GPA? +
LSAC GPA is a standardized recalculation of your undergraduate transcript that the Law School Admission Council performs for every law school applicant. It converts every grade to a common 4.33 scale, counts every repeated course attempt, and includes coursework from every undergraduate institution you attended. Your school GPA can differ because schools often use grade replacement, cap A+ at 4.0, or exclude transfer and community college credits that LSAC includes.
Is LSAC GPA the same as CAS GPA? +
Yes. CAS stands for Credential Assembly Service, the LSAC system that processes law school applications. LSAC GPA and CAS GPA both refer to the same recalculated number that appears on every applicant's Law School Report.
Does LSAC count retaken or repeated courses? +
Yes, LSAC includes both attempts if the course units and grades for the original attempt appear on the transcript. Grade replacement, academic renewal, and forgiveness policies at your school do not change how LSAC calculates your GPA.
Is an A+ really worth 4.33 on the LSAC scale? +
Yes. LSAC assigns an A+ a value of 4.33, which is higher than the 4.0 cap most universities use. Students whose schools award A plus grades can end up with an LSAC GPA above 4.0, even though their transcript GPA is capped at 4.0.
Does LSAC include community college or dual enrollment courses? +
Community college and transfer courses are included as long as they were taken before your first bachelor's degree was conferred. Dual enrollment courses taken in high school are also included for the 2026-2027 application cycle. Starting with the 2027-2028 cycle, LSAC will exclude dual enrollment grades from the GPA calculation.
What is changing about dual enrollment grades in 2027? +
LSAC originally announced it would stop counting high school dual enrollment grades starting July 28, 2026, then delayed that change to give candidates and schools more time to adjust. The exclusion now takes effect for the 2027-2028 application cycle and applies to new applicants and reapplicants alike. For the 2026-2027 cycle, dual enrollment grades are still included exactly as before.
Does pass/fail affect my LSAC GPA? +
Passing grades from a pass/fail system are excluded from the GPA calculation, though the credits are still recorded separately as unconverted credits. A failing grade in a pass/fail course is counted as zero and is included.
How are withdrawals handled, W, WP, and WF? +
A non-punitive withdrawal, usually shown as W or WP, is excluded from the GPA calculation. A punitive withdrawal that signifies failure, such as WF, is counted as zero grade points, the same as an F, unless your institution classifies it as non-punitive for a reason other than a successful repeat.
Does graduate school coursework count toward my LSAC GPA? +
No. Only coursework completed before your first bachelor's degree was conferred counts toward your LSAC GPA. Graduate, professional, and post-baccalaureate coursework is excluded, even though you must still submit those transcripts.
How do quarter credits convert to semester credits? +
LSAC converts quarter credit hours to semester credit hours by multiplying by 0.67. Trimester hours are treated the same as semester hours and need no conversion.
What if I have fewer than 60 graded credits? +
In certain cases involving fewer than 60 graded U.S. or Canadian undergraduate credits, LSAC does not calculate a numeric cumulative GPA and instead provides a qualitative academic summary. This mainly affects applicants with an international undergraduate degree or a transcript that shows only narrative grades.
How accurate is this LSAC GPA calculator? +
This calculator applies LSAC's published 4.33 grade scale and credit-weighted formula, so it is accurate for standard U.S. letter-grade transcripts. It cannot fully replicate LSAC's manual review of non-standard grading systems, foreign transcripts, or unusual transcript notations, so treat the result as a close estimate rather than your official CAS GPA.
