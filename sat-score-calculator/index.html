SCORING

How Digital SAT Scoring Works

The Digital SAT, used in the United States since March 2024, is shorter and adaptive compared with the old paper test. Your total score runs from 400 to 1600 and is the sum of two section scores, each from 200 to 800. Understanding how those scores are built is the key to using this calculator well and planning your prep.

The two sections and four modules

The test has two scored sections: Reading and Writing (RW) and Math. Each section is split into two modules, so you take four modules in total.

Adaptive Module 2

Module 1 contains a mix of easy, medium and hard questions. Your Module 1 performance decides whether Module 2 of that section is easier or harder, and each section is routed on its own.

  • Harder Module 2: unlocked by strong Module 1 performance. This path is the only one that can reach the top of the 200-800 scale.
  • Easier Module 2: given after weaker Module 1 performance. It limits your ceiling. College Board does not publish an exact cap, and about 600 per section is a commonly used estimate, which this calculator applies.

That is why Module 1 matters so much: a strong start earns points directly and also unlocks the higher scoring range for the whole section.

No guessing penalty: wrong answers do not subtract points on the Digital SAT. Answer every question, even if you have to guess.

Raw scores vs scaled scores

Your raw score is the number of questions you answered correctly (Reading and Writing: out of 54, Math: out of 44). Your scaled score is the 200-800 number colleges see. On the Digital SAT, College Board converts raw performance to a scaled score using item response theory (IRT), which takes question difficulty into account. Two students with the same number correct can therefore receive different scores, and College Board does not publish one fixed raw-to-scaled table.

That is why this tool is an estimator. It maps your number correct to a section score with three curves, so you can see a realistic range: generous (optimistic), average (most likely) and strict (conservative). Our calculator methodology page explains how the tools on this site are built.

HOW TO USE

How to Use This SAT Score Calculator

  1. Take a practice test under timed conditions, ideally an official College Board Bluebook practice test.
  2. Count your correct answers separately for Module 1 and Module 2 in each section.
  3. Enter the four numbers into the calculator above.
  4. Choose a curve. Start with Average. Use Strict for a conservative estimate or Generous for an optimistic one.
  5. Set Module 2 difficulty for Reading/Writing and for Math to match the versions you received.
  6. Read your results: estimated total, section scores, SAT percentile, ACT equivalent, and what the score means for admissions.

Run the estimate on more than one practice test. A single test can be a good or a bad day, and the trend across several tests is a better guide than any one result.

How to use your estimate to raise your score

  • Find your weaker section. Compare your Reading/Writing and Math scores and spend the extra study time where the gap is biggest.
  • Protect Module 1. It decides whether you reach the harder Module 2, so careless Module 1 mistakes cost the most.
  • Keep an error log. Write down why each missed question went wrong (content gap, careless error, or time) and review it weekly.
  • Set per-section targets with the goal planner above, so every practice session has a clear number to beat.
  • Plan your weeks. The Study Time Planner helps you fit SAT practice around school, and the GPA Calculator shows how your grades and score fit together.
  • Track the trend. Repeat a full timed practice test every one to two weeks and compare each new estimate with the last one.
PERCENTILES

Understanding Your SAT Percentile

A percentile shows the share of students you scored at or above. College Board publishes two kinds, and this calculator shows both:

  • SAT-taker (user) percentile: compares you with students who actually take the SAT. A 1200 is about the 75th percentile.
  • National percentile: compares you with all U.S. 11th and 12th graders, including students who never take the test. It is usually higher. A 1200 is about the 81st percentile.

Admissions officers usually think in terms of the SAT-taker pool, so that is the more useful number when you compare yourself with applicants. Use the percentile chart above for a quick reference, and see the College Board percentile tables for every score.

SUPERSCORING

What Is an SAT Superscore?

A superscore combines your highest Reading and Writing score and your highest Math score from different test dates. If you scored 700 RW and 650 Math in March, and 680 RW and 720 Math in May, your superscore is 700 + 720 = 1420, which is higher than either single sitting.

Not every college superscores, and policies on which scores you may send differ from school to school. Check each college's score-use policy before you plan retakes around a superscore.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the Digital SAT scored?+
The Digital SAT is scored from 400 to 1600. Reading and Writing and Math are each scored from 200 to 800, and the two section scores add up to your total. Each section has two modules, and College Board converts your answers to a scaled score using item response theory (IRT) rather than a simple count of correct answers. There is no penalty for wrong answers.
Is there an official raw score to scaled score chart for the Digital SAT?+
No. College Board does not publish a fixed raw-to-scaled conversion chart for the Digital SAT, because your score depends on which questions you answer correctly and which Module 2 you receive. The score chart on this page is an estimate built for planning, with three curves (average, generous and strict) so you can see a realistic range.
How does adaptive testing affect my SAT score?+
Module 1 of each section has mixed-difficulty questions. Based on your Module 1 performance, Module 2 is routed to an easier or harder set. The harder Module 2 can reach the top of the 200-800 scale. The easier Module 2 limits your ceiling. College Board does not publish an exact cap, and about 600 per section is a commonly used estimate, which this calculator applies.
How accurate is this SAT score calculator?+
It is a planning estimate, not an official score. The percentiles and the SAT-ACT conversion come from College Board data, but the raw-to-scaled step is modeled, because the real test uses IRT scoring and a different form every time. Expect your real score to differ from the estimate, and use the average, generous and strict curves to see a range.
How many questions can I miss and still get a 1500?+
On this calculator's average curve with a harder Module 2, a 1500 needs about 50 of 54 correct in Reading and Writing and about 40 of 44 correct in Math. That means missing roughly 4 questions in each section. Real tests vary, so use the goal planner above to check other scores and curves.
Is 1600 hard to get on the Digital SAT?+
Yes. A 1600 needs a perfect 800 in both sections, which means reaching the harder Module 2 in each section and answering almost every question correctly. This calculator's estimate requires every question to be correct for 1600. On real tests, a miss or two may or may not cost points depending on the form, so do not count on it.
Should I guess on the SAT?+
Yes. There is no penalty for wrong answers on the Digital SAT, so answer every question. A guess can only help you, and a blank can never earn a point.
What is a good SAT score?+
Recent College Board reports put the average total score at roughly 1025 to 1030. A 1200 is about the 75th percentile of SAT takers (81st nationally), a 1400 is about the 93rd (97th nationally), and a 1500 is about the 97th (99th nationally). What counts as good also depends on the colleges you are applying to.
What is the difference between SAT user percentile and national percentile?+
The SAT user percentile compares your score with students who actually take the SAT. The nationally representative percentile compares it with all U.S. 11th and 12th graders, including students who do not take the test, so it is usually higher. College Board publishes both. This calculator shows both.
What is SAT superscoring?+
Superscoring means a college combines your best Reading and Writing score and your best Math score across different test dates. For example, if you scored 700 RW and 650 Math in March, and 680 RW and 720 Math in May, your superscore is 700 + 720 = 1420. Policies vary by college, so check each school's score-use policy.
Is a 1480 SAT or a 34 ACT better?+
A 34 ACT is slightly higher. In the official 2018 ACT/SAT concordance, a 1480 SAT concords to an ACT 33, while an ACT 34 concords to about a 1500 SAT (range 1490 to 1520). Concordance is an estimate, and it can be off by about 2 ACT points.
Is a 1400 SAT enough for Harvard?+
A 1400 is below Harvard's middle-50% range, which recent Common Data Set figures put at roughly 1500 to 1580. A 1400 is still a strong score, around the 93rd percentile of SAT takers. Harvard reviews the whole application, and no score guarantees or rules out admission.
Is my data saved?+
No. The calculators run in your browser, and the scores you enter are not stored or shared.
