How Digital SAT Scoring Works

The Digital SAT, introduced in 2024, is shorter and fully adaptive compared to the old paper test. Understanding the scoring system is the key to using this calculator well and planning your prep.

The two sections

The test has two scored sections: Reading and Writing (RW) and Math. Each section is scored on a 200 to 800 scale, giving a total score between 400 and 1600. Your total is simply the two section scores added together.

Modules and adaptive routing

Each section is split into two modules. In Reading/Writing, each module has 27 questions (32 minutes each). In Math, each module has 22 questions (35 minutes each). Module 1 contains a mix of easy, medium, and hard questions. Based on how you perform, Module 2 is routed to one of two difficulty levels:

  • Harder Module 2: unlocked by strong Module 1 performance. This path allows scores up to 800 per section.
  • Easier Module 2: given after weaker Module 1 performance. Scores on this path are capped around 600 per section, regardless of how many you answer correctly.

That's why Module 1 matters so much. A strong start does not just earn points directly, it unlocks the higher scoring ceiling for the whole section.

No guessing penalty: wrong answers do not subtract points on the Digital SAT. Always answer every question, even if you have to guess. A random guess has a 25% chance of being right, while a blank has a 0% chance.

Raw scores vs scaled scores

Your raw score is the number of questions you answered correctly in a section (RW: out of 54, Math: out of 44). Your scaled score is the 200-800 number colleges see. The conversion between raw and scaled is done with a curve unique to each test administration, which accounts for slight difficulty differences between test forms. That's why this calculator offers three curves: generous, average, and strict.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Take a practice test under timed conditions, ideally an official College Board Bluebook practice test.
  2. Count your correct answers separately for Module 1 and Module 2 in each section.
  3. Enter the four numbers into the calculator above.
  4. Choose your curve. Start with the average curve. If your practice test felt easy, try the strict curve; if it felt hard, try the generous one.
  5. Set Module 2 difficulty to match the version you received (harder or easier).
  6. Read your results: estimated total, section scores, national percentile, ACT equivalent, and what your score means for admissions.

Understanding Your Percentile

Your percentile tells you what percentage of test takers you scored above. A 90th percentile means you outscored 90% of students. This calculator shows your national percentile based on College Board data, not a sample from a single prep company, so it reflects where you truly stand.

Percentiles matter because colleges evaluate your score in context. A 1400 might be the 94th percentile nationally, which tells an admissions officer far more than the raw number alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the Digital SAT scored?

The Digital SAT has two sections, Reading/Writing and Math, each scored from 200 to 800, for a total of 400 to 1600. Each section has two modules. Your raw score (number of correct answers) in each section is converted to a scaled score using a curve unique to that test. There is no penalty for wrong answers.

How does adaptive testing affect my SAT score?

Module 1 of each section contains mixed-difficulty questions. Based on your Module 1 performance, Module 2 is routed to an easier or harder set. The harder Module 2 allows scores up to 800 per section. The easier Module 2 caps your section score around 600, no matter how many you answer correctly.

How many questions can I miss and still get a 1500?

On an average-difficulty curve with harder Module 2 routing, you can typically miss about 4-5 questions in Reading/Writing and 2-3 in Math and still reach 1500. Use the goal planner above for exact numbers on each curve.

Is 1600 harder to get on the Digital SAT?

A perfect 1600 still requires near-perfect performance: typically all or all-but-one correct in each section on the harder Module 2 path. The adaptive format adds a routing hurdle, since you must perform well enough on Module 1 to unlock the harder Module 2.

Should I guess on the SAT?

Yes. There is no penalty for wrong answers on the Digital SAT, so answer every question. An educated guess has positive expected value compared to leaving a question blank.

What is a good SAT score?

The national average is around 1024. A score of 1200+ is above average, 1350+ is competitive for many flagship state universities, and 1500+ puts you in the top 1-2% and is competitive for highly selective schools.

What is SAT superscoring?

Superscoring means colleges combine your best section scores across multiple test dates. If you scored 700 RW + 650 Math in March and 680 RW + 720 Math in May, your superscore is 700 + 720 = 1420. Many colleges superscore the SAT.

How accurate is this SAT score calculator?

This calculator uses raw-to-scaled conversion tables modeled on official College Board Bluebook practice test curves. Real test curves vary slightly per administration, so treat results as estimates within about 20-40 points.

Is 1200 out of 1600 a good SAT score?

Yes, 1200 is a good SAT score. It is above the national average of about 1024 and lands around the 74th percentile, meaning you outscored roughly three-quarters of test takers. It's competitive for many state universities, though highly selective schools typically expect 1400+.

Is a 1480 SAT or 34 ACT better?

They are roughly equivalent. According to the official 2018 SAT-ACT concordance, a 1480 SAT concords to about a 33-34 ACT. Neither is objectively better; colleges treat concordant scores equally. Use the converter above for exact concordance.

Is 1400 SAT enough for Harvard?

A 1400 is below Harvard's middle-50% range of roughly 1490-1580, so it would be a weak score for Harvard specifically. However, Harvard looks at your whole application, and a 1400 is an excellent score nationally (around the 93rd percentile) that is competitive at many top-50 universities.

What is the most accurate SAT score calculator?

The most accurate calculators use raw-to-scaled conversion tables modeled on official College Board Bluebook practice test curves, account for adaptive Module 2 routing, and let you select the curve difficulty. This calculator does all three, with three curve options and explicit Module 2 difficulty input.
