ABOUT THE TOOL

What Is a Scholarship Calculator?

This college cost after scholarships calculator estimates how much you may need to pay after scholarships and grants are applied. It starts with your cost of attendance, subtracts gift aid to estimate net price, and keeps loans and work-study separate so borrowed or earned money is not presented as a discount.

Use the results for planning and comparison, then replace estimates with the figures in each school's official financial aid offer and net price calculator.

This is a planning estimate, not a scholarship eligibility test or an official financial aid offer. It does not determine whether you qualify for an award.

Estimated net price Cost of attendance − scholarships − grants = estimated net price
Remaining funding gap Estimated net price − family resources − expected work-study − accepted loans = funding gap

COLLEGE COSTS

What to Include in Cost of Attendance

College cost is broader than tuition. Use the same academic period for every input and include direct school charges plus realistic indirect expenses so your estimated net price is meaningful.

Tuition and required fees

Include published tuition, mandatory fees, program fees, and required enrollment charges.

Housing and food

Use the school's estimate or a realistic off-campus budget for rent, utilities, groceries, and meals.

Books and supplies

Include required books, course materials, lab supplies, equipment, and technology.

Transportation and personal costs

Estimate commuting, trips home, insurance, phone service, and other attendance-related expenses.

AID CATEGORIES

Scholarships, Grants, Work-Study, and Loans Are Different

Funding Type How It Works Included in Net Price?
Scholarship Gift aid based on merit, need, activity, employer support, or other criteria Yes
Grant Gift aid that normally does not require repayment Yes
Federal Work-Study Eligibility to earn wages through an approved job No
Student or Parent Loan Borrowed money that must be repaid, usually with interest No

Financial aid offers may group all four together. Separate gift aid from money that must be earned or repaid when comparing affordability.

RENEWAL PLANNING

Check Whether a Scholarship Is Renewable

A large first-year award can make a college look affordable even when part of the package does not continue. Check the maximum renewal period, annual amount, enrollment requirements, GPA rules, credit-completion conditions, and whether the award applies to all four years.

  • Enter one year for a one-time scholarship.
  • Do not assume an award automatically rises with tuition.
  • Check whether outside scholarships change institutional grants or other aid.
  • Use the projection table to identify the year an award ends.
COMPARE OFFERS

How to Compare College Financial Aid Offers

A larger scholarship does not automatically mean a lower college cost. Compare schools using the same cost categories and planning assumptions, then focus on net price and projected borrowing.

  • Start with full cost of attendance, not tuition alone.
  • Separate scholarships and grants from loans and work-study.
  • Check whether every award is renewable.
  • Estimate total borrowing across all years.
  • Use each college's official net price calculator before applying and its aid offer after admission.
TAX NOTE

Scholarships May Have Tax Considerations

Scholarship tax treatment can depend on the award, degree status, and how funds are used. Amounts used for qualified tuition, required fees, and required course materials may be treated differently from money used for housing, food, travel, optional equipment, or services.

This calculator does not estimate taxes, determine scholarship eligibility, or guarantee an aid award. Review current IRS guidance and your official financial aid documents when needed.
