ABOUT THE TOOL

What Is a Scholarship Calculator?

A Scholarship Calculator estimates how much college may cost after scholarships and grants are applied. It starts with cost of attendance, subtracts gift aid, and keeps loans and work-study separate so borrowed or earned money is not presented as a discount.

Use estimates while researching colleges, then replace them with the figures in each school's official financial aid offer.

Estimated net priceCost of attendance − scholarships − grants = estimated net price
Remaining funding gapEstimated net price − family resources − expected work-study − accepted loans = funding gap

COLLEGE COSTS

What to Include in Cost of Attendance

Cost of attendance is broader than tuition. Use the same academic period for every input and include direct school charges plus realistic indirect expenses.

Tuition and required fees

Include published tuition, mandatory fees, program fees, and required enrollment charges.

Housing and food

Use the school's estimate or a realistic off-campus budget for rent, utilities, groceries, and meals.

Books and supplies

Include required books, course materials, lab supplies, equipment, and technology.

Transportation and personal costs

Estimate commuting, trips home, insurance, phone service, and other attendance-related expenses.

AID CATEGORIES

Scholarships, Grants, Work-Study, and Loans Are Different

Funding TypeHow It WorksIncluded in Net Price?
ScholarshipGift aid based on merit, need, activity, employer support, or other criteriaYes
GrantGift aid that normally does not require repaymentYes
Federal Work-StudyEligibility to earn wages through an approved jobNo

Financial aid offers may group all four together. Separate gift aid from money that must be earned or repaid when comparing affordability.

RENEWAL PLANNING

Check Whether a Scholarship Is Renewable

A large first-year award can make a college look affordable even when part of the package does not continue. Check the maximum renewal period, annual amount, enrollment requirements, GPA rules, and credit-completion conditions.

  • Enter one year for a one-time scholarship.
  • Do not assume an award automatically rises with tuition.
  • Check whether outside scholarships change institutional grants or other aid.
  • Use the projection table to identify the year an award ends.
COMPARE OFFERS

How to Compare College Financial Aid Offers

A larger scholarship does not automatically mean a lower college cost. Compare schools using the same cost categories and planning assumptions.

  • Start with full cost of attendance, not tuition alone.
  • Separate scholarships and grants from loans and work-study.
  • Check whether every award is renewable.
  • Estimate total borrowing across all years.
  • Use each college's official net price calculator before applying and its aid offer after admission.
TAX NOTE

Scholarships May Have Tax Considerations

Scholarship tax treatment can depend on the award, degree status, and how funds are used. Amounts used for qualified tuition, required fees, and required course materials may be treated differently from money used for housing, food, travel, optional equipment, or services.

This Scholarship Calculator does not estimate taxes. Review current IRS guidance and obtain professional advice when needed.
