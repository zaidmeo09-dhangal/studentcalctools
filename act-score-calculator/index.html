How ACT scoring works

Your ACT score starts as a raw score: the number of questions you answered correctly. There is no penalty for wrong answers, so a blank answer and a wrong answer cost the same, and guessing is never worse than leaving a question empty.

ACT then converts each raw score to a scale score from 1 to 36. The conversion is not one fixed chart. ACT equates every test form separately, so the same raw score can convert slightly differently from one test date to another. That is why every calculator, including this one, gives you an estimate of about one point when you start from raw answers.

On the Enhanced ACT, your Composite is the average of three scale scores: English, Math and Reading, rounded to the nearest whole number. Science no longer counts toward the Composite. If you take Science, it still gets its own 1 to 36 score, and it combines with Math into a separate STEM score. Writing stays separate too, reported from 2 to 12, and never changes your Composite.

Students who tested before September 2025 were scored the Legacy way: the Composite averaged all four sections, Science included. Those scores are still valid and still reported by ACT, so the Legacy mode above exists for checking an older score report.

A benchmark is the score at which a student has a 50% chance of earning a B or higher, and about a 75 to 80% chance of earning a C or higher, in the matching first-year college course. Falling short does not close doors, but it tells you exactly where a retake gains you the most.

Superscore, ACT vs SAT, and next test dates

If you have taken the ACT more than once, many colleges will superscore you: they take your best section scores across all your sittings and build a new Composite from those. A 24 in English from March and a 29 in Math from June count together. Use the superscore block above to see yours. Policies differ by college, so always confirm on the college own testing page, and remember that some colleges ask for one full sitting instead.

Colleges weigh ACT and SAT scores equally, so the better test is the one that fits how you work: the ACT moves faster and includes a Science section, the SAT gives you more time per question. On the official concordance the two tests publish together, an ACT 30 lines up with about SAT 1370, an ACT 25 with about SAT 1210, and an ACT 20 with about SAT 1040. If you have an SAT score instead, run it through our SAT score calculator to compare properly.

Frequently asked questions

How is the ACT Composite score calculated?+
On the Enhanced ACT, add your English, Math and Reading scale scores and divide by 3, rounding to the nearest whole number (one-half rounds up). Science and Writing never enter the Composite. On tests before September 2025, the Legacy formula averaged all four sections including Science.
What is a good ACT score in 2026?+
It depends on your colleges, but the current national picture is: 28 or above puts you in the top 10% of recent graduates, 24 to 27 is competitive for state flagships and many merit scholarships, and 20 to 23 sits around the national average of 19.1. The most selective colleges usually admit students in the 32 to 36 range.
How many questions are on the Enhanced ACT?+
The core test has 131 questions: 50 English, 45 Math and 36 Reading, plus 40 more if you take the optional Science section, 171 in total. Not all of them count: 40 English, 41 Math, 27 Reading and 34 Science questions are scored, and the rest are field test questions ACT is trialling.
Why does the calculator ask for fewer questions than the test has?+
Because only some questions are scored. The rest are field test items that do not count toward your score, and their position changes between test forms. If your practice test answer key marks the scored questions, use the Scored questions only mode for the closest estimate. If you counted every correct answer, use Practice test mode and treat the result as an estimate.
Do I need to take the ACT Science section?+
For the test itself, no: Science is optional on the Enhanced ACT and does not affect your Composite. Take it if you want a STEM score, if a college or program you like asks for it, or if your school or state testing program includes it. When in doubt, check the requirements of the colleges on your list.
Is there a penalty for wrong answers on the ACT?+
No. Your raw score is simply the number of correct answers, so a wrong answer and a blank answer score the same. Never leave a question empty: a guess can only help.
How accurate is this calculator?+
If you enter official section scores, the Composite, percentile and superscore math is exact. From raw answers it is an estimate of about one point, because ACT converts raw scores separately for every test form and only publishes sample conversion tables. This calculator uses the conversion table from ACT own official practice test scoring key.
What is the ACT STEM score?+
STEM is the average of your Math and Science scale scores, rounded, reported from 1 to 36. It only exists if you take Science. The College Readiness Benchmark for STEM is 26. It never changes your Composite.
What is the ELA score, and does Writing affect my Composite?+
ELA is the rounded average of your English, Reading and Writing scores, and it only exists if you take Writing. Your Writing score itself runs from 2 to 12 and is reported separately. Neither ELA nor Writing ever changes your Composite.
What is an ACT superscore, and do colleges accept it?+
A superscore combines your best section scores across multiple test dates into one Composite. Many colleges superscore the ACT, but policies vary and some require a single sitting, so check each college testing policy. The superscore block on this page works yours out from up to four sittings.
Can I still use an ACT score from before September 2025?+
Yes. Legacy scores were calculated with Science inside the Composite, and ACT still reports them. Colleges accept them the same way; use the Legacy mode on this page to recheck the math on an older report.
What ACT score do I need for a scholarship?+
There is no single national cutoff. Many colleges publish automatic merit scholarships with ACT bands on their own scholarship pages, and the bands often start in the mid-20s. Find the published band for each college on your list, set it as your target in the goal planner on this page, and estimate the money side with our scholarship calculator.
How does the ACT compare to the SAT?+
On the official concordance published by ACT and the College Board, an ACT Composite of 36 matches about SAT 1590, 30 matches about 1370, 25 matches about 1210 and 20 matches about 1040. Colleges treat the two tests equally, so choose the format that suits you and compare scores with the concordance, not by feel.
