**For class rank reporting on the Common App, select the method your school officially uses: Exact, Decile, Quintile, Quartile or None. Check your transcript, school profile or counselor’s instructions. If your school does not rank students, choose None.**

The options describe how your school reports academic standing. They are not choices you make based on which answer looks strongest.

If you already have an official numerical rank and class size, you can [convert your rank](https://studentcalctools.com/class-rank-calculator/) into a top percentage and percentile estimate. Use those results to understand your standing, while keeping your school’s official information for the application.



## **What Does Class Rank Reporting Mean?**

Class rank reporting means the format your school uses to describe your academic position within your graduating class.

Your school might report an exact position, such as 23rd out of 244 students. It might instead report a group, such as the highest 10% or highest 25%. Some schools do not report individual rank.

The Common App’s five options accommodate those different practices.


|  |  |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| **Your school officially reports** | **Common App option** |
| A numerical position, such as 23 out of 244 | Exact |
| A ranking band in groups of 10% | Decile |
| A ranking band in groups of 20% | Quintile |
| A ranking band in groups of 25% | Quartile |
| No individual class rank | None |


A missing rank on one document does not automatically mean you should choose None. Your counselor can confirm whether the school reports rank elsewhere.



## **Where Is Class Rank Reporting in the Common App?**

Look in the **Education section**, alongside your grades and academic information.

The [official Common App high-school details](https://commonapp.org/files/fy-toolkit/High-School-Details-Sheet.pdf) sheet identifies these related items:

- Graduating class size (approx.).
- Class rank reporting.
- Class rank weighting.
- GPA scale.
- GPA weighting.

The worksheet lists **Exact, Decile, Quintile, Quartile and None** as the class rank reporting options.

Additional questions may appear depending on your selections. Follow the wording shown in your application and enter the corresponding school-issued information.

Your counselor also provides rank information through the School Report. Your student entries should agree with the school’s reporting method.



## **Which Common App Class Rank Option Should You Select?**

Select the format your school reports for you. An exact rank and a ranking band can describe the same student, but that does not make them interchangeable on the application.

### **Exact**

Choose **Exact** when your school officially reports a specific numerical position.

Examples include:

- Rank 23 out of 244.
- Rank 12 out of 350.
- Rank 4 out of 180.

If your official rank is 23 out of 244, your rank number is **23** and your class size is **244**.

Do not enter your GPA or top percentage in the numerical rank field.

If your school assigns tied ranks, use the position it officially reports. Do not invent a different position to separate yourself from other students.

### **Decile**

Choose **Decile** when your school reports standing in ten groups, each covering approximately 10% of the class.

Under a top-down reporting convention:

- First decile means the highest 10%.
- Second decile means the next 10%, outside the highest 10% but within the highest 20%.
- Third decile means the next 10%, outside the highest 20% but within the highest 30%.

Use the band your school assigns. Confirm its definitions if the numbering is unclear.

A school that reports “top 10%” may be using decile reporting, but ask your counselor when the label does not explicitly identify the method.

### **Quintile**

Choose **Quintile** when your school reports standing in five groups of approximately 20%.

Under the top-down convention:

- First quintile means the highest 20%.
- Second quintile covers the next 20%.
- The remaining quintiles continue through the class.

A quintile gives a broader range than a decile. First quintile alone does not establish that a student is in the highest 10%.

### **Quartile**

Choose **Quartile** when your school reports standing in four groups of approximately 25%.

Under the top-down convention:

- First quartile means the highest 25%.
- Second quartile covers the next 25%.
- Third and fourth quartiles cover the remaining half.

Quartile and quintile are different. The highest quartile covers 25% of the class; the highest quintile covers 20%.

### **None**

Choose **None** if your school does not rank students.

This does not mean that your GPA is zero, that your grades are poor, or that you are at the bottom of the class. It indicates that individual class rank is not reported.

If you cannot find your rank, first confirm the school’s policy. “I do not know my rank” and “my school does not rank” are different situations.



## **Worked Example: Rank 23 Out of 244 in Every Format**

Suppose a school officially reports a student’s exact rank as **23 out of 244**.

For this example, assume distinct ranks and a simple top-down grouping method. Real schools may use different rules for ties and group boundaries.

First, calculate the student’s top percentage:

**Top percentage = (Rank ÷ Class size) × 100**

Top percentage = (23 ÷ 244) × 100

Top percentage = approximately **9.43%**

That position falls within the highest 10%, and therefore also within the highest 20% and highest 25%.


|  |  |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Reporting format** | **How this position can be described** |
| Exact | 23rd out of 244 |
| Decile | First decile: within the highest 10% |
| Quintile | First quintile: within the highest 20% |
| Quartile | First quartile: within the highest 25% |
| None | Not appropriate for this example, because the school officially reports the student’s rank |


The calculation explains the relationship between the formats. It does not authorize the student to switch reporting methods.

**If the school officially reports 23 out of 244, select Exact.** Do not replace it with Decile simply because “top 10%” sounds more appealing.

If another school officially reports only a quartile, its student should report that quartile rather than inventing an exact rank.

### **What Would This Student Enter?**

If the example school reports the rank as exact and confirms that it is weighted:


|  |  |
| ------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Information requested** | **Example response** |
| Class rank reporting | Exact |
| Numerical rank | 23 |
| Graduating class size | 244 |
| Rank weighting | Weighted |


The weighting label comes from the school’s calculation. It cannot be determined from 23/244.

Also, **9.43% is the student’s calculated top percentage, not their numerical rank**. It should not replace 23 in a field asking for an exact position.



## **How Do You Find Your School’s Reporting Method?**

Start with your current transcript, then check the school profile and any Common App instructions supplied by your counseling office.

Look for wording such as:

- Class Rank: 23/244.
- First Decile.
- Top 20%.
- First Quartile.
- School does not rank.

For a fuller walkthrough, see [where to find your class rank.](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/what-is-my-class-rank/)

If the answer remains unclear, ask your counselor:

“Which class rank reporting option should I select on the Common App, what rank or band should I enter, and is that rank weighted or unweighted?”

Also confirm the class size and reporting date attached to that rank.

### **What If Your Transcript and Portal Disagree?**

Do not combine a rank from one reporting period with a class size from another.

For example:

- An older transcript shows 23 out of 244.
- A newer portal entry shows 21 out of 241.

Ask your counselor which pair is current and official for application reporting.

### **What If Your School Profile Shows GPA Deciles?**

A school profile may publish GPA ranges for the graduating class without assigning an individual rank to every student.

For example, a profile might show the GPA range associated with the highest decile. Comparing your GPA with that range can help you understand the distribution, but it does not automatically establish an official rank you should enter.

Ask whether your school assigns and reports an individual decile for you.



## **What Does Graduating Class Size Mean?**

Graduating class size means the number of students in your graduating cohort. It is not the total enrollment of the high school or the size of one classroom.

For an exact rank of 23 out of 244:

- 23 is the student’s position.
- 244 is the size of the ranked group.

The Common App worksheet labels this information **“Graduating class size (approx.).”** Use the figure your school provides.

If your rank is based on an unusual group, such as a district-wide cohort or a specific academic program, ask your counselor how to report the class size consistently.

Choosing None for rank does not mean your graduating class size is zero. Your school can provide class-size information even when it does not rank students.



## **What Is Rank Weighting on the Common App?**

Rank weighting identifies whether the reported rank is based on a weighted or unweighted academic calculation.


|  |  |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rank weighting** | **Meaning** |
| Weighted | The ranking calculation incorporates the school’s approved weighting for eligible advanced coursework |
| Unweighted | The ranking calculation does not include course-difficulty bonuses |


The school determines which courses qualify and how weighting works. Taking AP, IB or honors courses does not, by itself, prove that your reported rank is weighted.

For a worked comparison, read [weighted vs unweighted rank.](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/weighted-vs-unweighted-class-rank/)

### **Is Rank Weighting the Same as GPA Weighting?**

They are separate questions.

Rank weighting describes the calculation behind your reported class position. GPA weighting describes the GPA value you report.

Check each label independently. Do not assume the rank uses the same weighting as whichever GPA happens to appear nearby on your transcript.

A GPA of 4.30 is a grade-point average. A weighted rank of 23 out of 244 is a position. The application needs the correct type of information in each field.

### **What If Your School Calculates Both Rankings?**

[Common App’s official counselor guidance](https://www.commonapp.org/files/webinar_decks/Counselor_Convo_International.pdf) says to report the weighted value when the school calculates both weighted and unweighted class rank/GPA.

Use the actual weighted rank supplied by your school and identify it as Weighted.

For example, if your official records show:

- Weighted rank: 23 out of 244.
- Unweighted rank: 31 out of 244.

Use the weighted position, 23, under that guidance. Do not pair the unweighted position with the Weighted label.



## **What Should You Do If Your School Does Not Rank?**

Select **None** for class rank reporting, and complete the other academic information according to your school’s records.

You can still report the GPA and class-size information your school provides. A school can calculate GPA without assigning individual class ranks.

Your counselor’s materials can help explain the academic environment. [Common App’s school-profile guidance](https://commonapp.org/files/recommender-resources/Resource_REC_School_Profile_ENG_2025.07.08.pdf) describes how profiles provide context about grading systems, curriculum and school policies.

If the school calculates rank internally but does not normally release it, ask your counselor what it officially reports for applications. Do not substitute an unofficial estimate.

A GPA-based calculator estimate is useful for personal understanding, but it should not become an official Common App rank.



## **Does Choosing None Hurt Your Application?**

**If your school does not rank, None is the accurate response. It does not describe weak academic performance.**

Colleges can review the grades, courses and school information that are available. Their policies are not identical, so avoid treating one universal promise about rank as an admissions rule.

For a verified example, [Providence College explicitly states](https://admission.providence.edu/apply/review-process/) that students are not penalized in its admission review when their high school does not provide class rank information.

A separate scholarship, honors program or rank-based admission pathway may have its own documentation requirements. Check those requirements directly if they apply to you.

If your school does report an official rank, do not select None merely because you would prefer to omit the number. Confirm the reporting policy with your counselor.



## **Check Your Class Rank Entries Before Submitting**

Review these details together:

- **Reporting method:** Does your selection match the school’s official format?
- **Rank or band:** Did you enter the school-issued position or grouping?
- **Class size:** Does it match the cohort associated with that rank?
- **Weighting:** Does the label match the calculation behind the rank?
- **Reporting period:** Are the numbers current and from the same record?
- **Counselor information:** Will your entries agree with the School Report?

Common App’s guidance says to report your current rank and GPA even if they may change later in the school year.

If you find a discrepancy, resolve it with your counselor before submitting rather than trying to reconcile the numbers yourself.
