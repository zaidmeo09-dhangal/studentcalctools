**To calculate CGPA, multiply each semester GPA by that semester’s GPA credits, add the results, and divide by the total GPA credits. This gives semesters with more credits the correct weight.**

**CGPA = Sum of (Semester GPA × Semester GPA credits) ÷ Total GPA credits**

If your transcript lists total quality points, you can use the more direct formula:

**CGPA = Total quality points ÷ Total GPA credits**

Use the [CGPA calculator](https://studentcalctools.com/cgpa-calculator/) to combine your semester GPAs and credits. The four-semester example below shows exactly how the calculation works.



## **What Is CGPA?**

CGPA stands for cumulative grade point average. It combines the grades included in your cumulative academic record into one average.

For example, your fall semester GPA describes your performance during that term. Your cumulative GPA after the following spring semester combines the included coursework from both terms.

As you complete more coursework, your cumulative GPA incorporates those additional grades and credits.

The scope matters. An institutional GPA may include only coursework taken at one college, while another reported GPA may include transfer work. Use the definition attached to the GPA you are calculating.



## **CGPA vs. Semester GPA: What Is the Difference?**

Semester GPA covers one term. CGPA covers the coursework included across your cumulative record.


| Term | What it describes |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Semester GPA | Your GPA for one semester |
| Term GPA | Your GPA for one academic term, such as a semester or quarter |
| CGPA or cumulative GPA | Your GPA across the included cumulative record |
| GPA | A general term that can refer to a semester, cumulative, or other defined GPA |


The word “GPA” alone does not always mean semester GPA. Check the label on your transcript or application.

For a fuller comparison, read [GPA vs CGPA](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/gpa-vs-cgpa/).



## **What You Need Before Calculating CGPA**

Gather the following from your transcript or academic record:

1. Each semester’s GPA.
2. The GPA credits used to calculate that semester’s GPA.
3. Your school’s grading scale.
4. Any adjustments for repeated courses, grade replacement, or other exclusions.

Use a separate term GPA for each semester. Do not enter the running cumulative GPA shown after every term as though it were a semester GPA. That would count earlier coursework more than once.

All values must also use a consistent GPA scale and credit system.

### **GPA Credits Are Not Always the Same as Earned Credits**

GPA credits are the credits included in the GPA calculation. They may be labeled GPA hours or another similar term on your transcript.

Earned credits measure something different: credits you have received toward academic progress.

For example:

- A failed letter-graded course may contribute GPA credits and zero quality points, even though you earned no credit for passing it.
- A passing grade in a course excluded from GPA may earn degree credit without contributing GPA credits.
- Transfer credits may count toward your degree while being excluded from your institutional GPA.

The [University of Illinois GPA guide](https://registrar.illinois.edu/courses-grades/calculate-your-gpa/) illustrates this distinction: its example includes GPA hours for an F but excludes S and CR entries from the GPA calculation.

Using all earned credits as the denominator can therefore produce the wrong result.



## **The CGPA Formula Explained**

The most direct formula is:

**CGPA = Total quality points ÷ Total GPA credits**

Quality points measure the grade points earned across a course’s credits.

For a course:

**Course quality points = Grade-point value × Course credits**

Suppose your school assigns 4.0 grade points to an A. An A in a three-credit course produces:

Quality points = 4.0 × 3 = 12

Add the quality points from all included courses, then divide by their total GPA credits.

The [University of Notre Dame registrar](https://registrar.nd.edu/exams-grading/grades/calculate-gpa/) explains cumulative GPA using this total-quality-points approach.

### **Calculating CGPA From Semester GPAs**

When you have semester GPAs and their matching GPA credits, use:

**CGPA = Sum of (Semester GPA × Semester GPA credits) ÷ Total GPA credits**

For two semesters, this becomes:

**CGPA = [(Semester 1 GPA × Semester 1 GPA credits) + (Semester 2 GPA × Semester 2 GPA credits)] ÷ Combined GPA credits**

Multiplying a semester GPA by its GPA credits recovers that term’s quality points when the GPA is exact.

If the displayed semester GPA has been rounded or truncated, the result is an estimate. Use the transcript’s actual quality-point totals when available.



## **How to Calculate CGPA of All Semesters: A Four-Semester Example**

Suppose a student has completed four terms on the same 4.0 scale.

For this example, assume the semester GPAs are exact, every listed credit counts toward GPA, and there are no repeat-course or grade-replacement adjustments.


| Semester | Semester GPA | GPA credits | Quality points: GPA × credits |
| ----------- | ------------ | ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| Fall 2024 | 3.00 | 15 | 45.00 |
| Spring 2025 | 3.50 | 12 | 42.00 |
| Summer 2025 | 4.00 | 6 | 24.00 |
| Fall 2025 | 3.20 | 15 | 48.00 |
| Total | — | 48 | 159.00 |


### **Step 1: Multiply Each Semester GPA by Its GPA Credits**

Fall 2024: 3.00 × 15 = 45.00

Spring 2025: 3.50 × 12 = 42.00

Summer 2025: 4.00 × 6 = 24.00

Fall 2025: 3.20 × 15 = 48.00

### **Step 2: Add the Quality Points**

Total quality points = 45 + 42 + 24 + 48 = 159

### **Step 3: Add the GPA Credits**

Total GPA credits = 15 + 12 + 6 + 15 = 48

### **Step 4: Divide Quality Points by GPA Credits**

CGPA = 159 ÷ 48 = 3.3125

Rounded to two decimal places for this example:

**CGPA = 3.31**

Your school may use a different display rule, so retain the full result until the final step.

### **How the Cumulative GPA Changes After Each Term**

Using the same record:


| After this term | Cumulative quality points | Cumulative GPA credits | CGPA, rounded to two decimals |
| --------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Fall 2024 | 45 | 15 | 3.00 |
| Spring 2025 | 87 | 27 | 3.22 |
| Summer 2025 | 111 | 33 | 3.36 |
| Fall 2025 | 159 | 48 | 3.31 |


The summer 4.00 raises the cumulative average, but its six credits have less influence than a 15-credit semester.

The following 3.20 semester lowers the cumulative average slightly because it is below the student’s previous cumulative GPA.



## **Why You Should Not Simply Average Semester GPAs**

A simple average gives every semester equal weight, even when the credit loads differ.

Using the four semesters above:

Simple average = (3.00 + 3.50 + 4.00 + 3.20) ÷ 4

Simple average = 3.425

Rounded to two decimal places, that gives 3.43.

But the correct credit-weighted result is 3.31.


| Method | Result, rounded to two decimals | What it does |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Simple average of semester GPAs | 3.43 | Treats every term equally |
| Credit-weighted cumulative GPA | 3.31 | Weights each term by its GPA credits |


The simple average gives the six-credit summer term as much influence as each 15-credit term. That overstates the cumulative GPA in this example.

A simple average of semester GPAs works when every semester has the same number of GPA credits, subject to rounding and school-specific adjustments.



## **How to Calculate CGPA of Two Semesters**

Multiply each semester GPA by its GPA credits, add the results, and divide by the combined GPA credits.

Using the first two terms from the example:

- Semester 1: 3.00 GPA across 15 GPA credits.
- Semester 2: 3.50 GPA across 12 GPA credits.

Semester 1 quality points = 3.00 × 15 = 45

Semester 2 quality points = 3.50 × 12 = 42

Combined quality points = 45 + 42 = 87

Combined GPA credits = 15 + 12 = 27

**CGPA = 87 ÷ 27 = approximately 3.22**

A simple average would give 3.25, which is different because the semesters carry different credit loads.



## **What If You Have Course Grades Instead of Semester GPAs?**

Start by calculating each course’s quality points.

1. Find the grade-point value assigned to each grade by your school.
2. Multiply that value by the course’s GPA credits.
3. Add the quality points.
4. Divide by the total included GPA credits.

You can combine all included courses directly. You do not have to calculate a GPA for each semester first.

Use the [GPA calculator](https://studentcalctools.com/gpa-calculator/) for course-level calculations, and see [how to calculate your GPA](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/how-to-calculate-gpa/) for a detailed walkthrough.

Check that the calculator’s grade-point values match your school’s scale, especially for plus and minus grades.



## **Why Your Calculation Might Differ From Your Transcript**

A correct formula can still produce a different number if your inputs do not match the registrar’s rules.

### **Rounded Semester GPAs**

A displayed semester GPA may omit decimals used in the official calculation.

Multiplying that shortened number by credits can produce an approximate quality-point total. Use official quality points when your transcript provides them.

### **Pass/Fail Courses**

Do not assume every completed credit belongs in your GPA denominator.

Suppose you have:

- 36 quality points.
- 12 GPA credits.
- Another three credits from a passing course that your school excludes from GPA.

The calculation is:

CGPA = 36 ÷ 12 = 3.00

Dividing by all 15 credits would give 2.40, which would be incorrect under those assumptions.

Rules depend on the institution and grading option. [Georgia Tech’s GPA policy](https://registrar.gatech.edu/info/grade-point-average-calculation), for example, excludes pass/fail and audited courses from its GPA calculation.

### **Repeated Courses and Grade Replacement**

Do not automatically remove an earlier grade when you repeat a course.

Georgia Tech generally includes both attempts unless its grade-substitution policy applies. Other institutions may use different rules.

If an earlier grade has been excluded from your cumulative GPA, old semester GPA figures may not reconstruct the adjusted result. Use the current transcript totals.

### **Transfer Coursework**

Transferred credit does not always bring its grades into the receiving school’s GPA.

Georgia Tech excludes transfer grades from its institutional GPA. Illinois separately reports institutional, transfer, and overall GPAs.

Decide which GPA you need before combining records from different schools.

### **Rounding and Truncation**

This article rounds example results to two decimal places unless stated otherwise. Your institution may do something different.

Illinois truncates its reported GPA to two decimal places. Its published example shows 2.1875 as 2.18 rather than 2.19.

Follow your registrar’s rule when reproducing an official result.

### **Different GPA Scales**

Do not combine GPAs from different scales without an approved conversion method.

For example, [MIT calculates GPA on a 5.0 scale](https://registrar.mit.edu/classes-grades-evaluations/grades/calculating-gpa). A GPA on that scale cannot simply be mixed with semester GPAs reported on a 4.0 scale.

For high school records, keep weighted and unweighted GPA calculations separate unless your school provides a method for combining them.



## **How to Raise Your CGPA**

Under an unchanged calculation policy, a new semester GPA above your current CGPA raises the cumulative average. A semester GPA below it lowers the average.

How much it changes depends on the number of credits you have already accumulated and the number you add.

Use:

**New CGPA = (Current quality points + New semester quality points) ÷ (Current GPA credits + New semester GPA credits)**

Return to the student with 159 quality points across 48 GPA credits. Suppose the next semester contains 15 GPA credits.


| Next semester GPA | New semester quality points | New CGPA, rounded to two decimals |
| ----------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 2.50 | 37.50 | 3.12 |
| 3.00 | 45.00 | 3.24 |
| 3.50 | 52.50 | 3.36 |
| 4.00 | 60.00 | 3.48 |


These projections assume all 15 new credits count toward GPA and no earlier grades are changed or excluded.

### **Calculate the Semester GPA Needed for a Target**

You can also work backward from a target cumulative GPA:

**Required semester GPA = [(Target CGPA × Total GPA credits after next semester) − Current quality points] ÷ Next semester GPA credits**

Suppose the same student wants a 3.40 CGPA after the next 15-credit semester.

Total GPA credits after next semester = 48 + 15 = 63

Required total quality points = 3.40 × 63 = 214.20

Additional quality points needed = 214.20 − 159 = 55.20

Required semester GPA = 55.20 ÷ 15 = 3.68

The student needs a **3.68 semester GPA** under these assumptions.

For a target CGPA of 3.50:

Required semester GPA = [(3.50 × 63) − 159] ÷ 15 = 4.10

If the school’s maximum semester GPA is 4.00, that target is not reachable in this one 15-credit semester without some other adjustment to the existing record.
