**Texas's Top 10% Rule, officially House Bill 588, guarantees automatic admission to each general academic teaching institution in Texas for students who graduate in the top 10 percent of their high school class, meet the curriculum or test condition, and apply by the campus deadline. Private universities are not covered by this rule.** The University of Texas at Austin is a legal exception to that number: its actual current cutoff is the top 5 percent, not 10, and not the top 6 percent still cited on many older pages. Most other Texas public universities, including Texas A&M, still use something close to the original top 10 percent. This guide to the Texas top 10 percent rule covers exactly how it works, why UT Austin's number is different, and how to check whether your own rank qualifies, starting with the [class rank calculator](https://studentcalctools.com/class-rank-calculator/) if you want to check your percentile before reading further.



## **What the Rule Actually Is**

House Bill 588, commonly called the Top 10% Rule, was signed into Texas law on May 20, 1997 by then-Governor George W. Bush (Ballotpedia, [Texas House Bill 588, the "Top 10% Rule" (1997)](https://ballotpedia.org/Texas_House_Bill_588,_the_%22Top_10%25_Rule%22_(1997))). It was passed the year after *Hopwood v. Texas*, a federal appeals court ruling that struck down affirmative action in admissions at the University of Texas School of Law. Lawmakers built a race-neutral alternative instead: guarantee admission based on class rank, a measure that doesn't reference race directly but still broadens which high schools feed into the state's flagship universities.

The law itself is straightforward. Each general academic teaching institution must automatically admit an applicant who graduated in the top 10 percent of their class, within the two school years before the application year, from an accredited Texas public or private high school, a Department of Defense high school, or through nontraditional secondary education, who meets the curriculum or test condition, and who applies by the campus deadline. UT Austin is test-required, so test scores do matter there.



## **UT Austin Is the Exception, and the Number Keeps Changing**

For most Texas public universities, the rule works exactly as written: top 10 percent, automatic admission, no cap. UT Austin is different. By the mid-2000s, so many applicants qualified under the straight top 10 percent rule that the university had very little room left to admit anyone through any other process, a Texas Senate bill analysis from 2007 noted that 71 percent of UT Austin's entire 2006 freshman class had been admitted automatically under the rule (Texas Legislature, [Senate Bill 1569 Analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/80R/analysis/html/SB01569I.htm)).

In response, the legislature passed Senate Bill 175 in 2009, giving UT Austin, and only UT Austin, permission to not automatically admit beyond the number that fills 75 percent of the enrollment capacity designated for first-time resident undergraduates. Offers go by percentile from the top down, based on the prior year's applicant data, and the cutoff is reported to Texas high schools for use by current juniors. Texans in the top 6 to 10 percent who miss the UT cutoff go to holistic review under section 51.805, they are not automatically admitted and they are not automatically rejected.

That cutoff has tightened over time as demand for UT Austin has grown:


| Period | UT Austin Automatic Admission Cutoff |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| 1997 to 2009 | Top 10% (uncapped, same as statewide rule) |
| Multiple years through Spring 2026 | Top 6% |
| Summer/Fall 2026 through Spring 2028 | **Top 5%** |


The top 5 percent figure is confirmed directly on UT Austin's own admissions page, current as of this article's publish date (University of Texas at Austin, [Review & Decision Process](https://admissions.utexas.edu/apply/review-decision-process/)). If you've seen top 6 percent cited elsewhere, that was accurate for prior cycles but is no longer the number to plan around.



## **Which Other Schools Use the Rule**

UT Austin's tighter cutoff is the exception, not the norm. Most other Texas public universities still use something close to the original top 10 percent threshold:

- **Texas A&M University** uses the standard top 10 percent cutoff, on or before the deadline, for a Texas resident or a student enrolled in a recognized Texas public, private, or homeschool program. Admission to a major is not guaranteed. Texas A&M is test-optional and requires Essay Topic A. The Fall 2027 application deadline is Dec 1, 2026.
- **Texas Tech** uses the top 10 percent standard with no test minimum for top 10 percent students. Outside the top 10 percent, the assured levels are: 1st quarter ACT 24 or SAT 1180, 2nd quarter ACT 26 or SAT 1240, 3rd quarter ACT 27 or SAT 1280, 4th quarter review only. Rawls College of Business, Whitacre College of Engineering, and some science programs take assured students only.
- **University of Houston** uses top 10 percent, or a 3.5 or higher UH core GPA, or SAT 1250 or ACT 26. Other applicants go to essay plus resume review. The Fall 2027 deadline is May 31, 2027. The UH assured simplification is effective Aug 1, 2026.
- **Texas State, University of North Texas**, and most other Texas public universities follow the top 10 percent floor. Check each school's own published page for its exact current threshold for your application year.
- **UT System schools other than UT Austin** (UT San Antonio, UT Arlington, UT El Paso, UT Dallas, and others) generally follow the standard top 10 percent rule, not UT Austin's special cap.

If you're applying to more than one Texas public university, don't assume the same rank that qualifies you at one school qualifies you everywhere, check each school's specific published threshold.

## Do you need the Distinguished Level of Achievement to qualify?

Yes, normally. Under TEC 51.803(a)(2)(A), the auto-admit curriculum condition is the Distinguished Level of Achievement, unless the THECB test-score alternative applies or a required course was unavailable for reasons outside the student's control.

| Plan | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation | 22 credits |
| Endorsement | 26 credits total |
| Distinguished | Foundation plus endorsement plus Algebra II, with 4 math and 4 science credits |

Foundation-only, 22 credits with no endorsement, does not satisfy the auto-admit curriculum condition, although Texas Tech notes it still allows applying.



## **The Homeschool and Non-Traditional Pathway**

Students who don't have a traditional class rank, most commonly homeschooled students, qualify for UT Austin's automatic admission differently. Under House Bill 3041 (2025), which amended the relevant section of the Texas Education Code, non-traditional secondary education applicants for Spring 2027, Summer/Fall 2027, and Spring 2028 qualify with an SAT Composite score of 1570 or higher, or an ACT Composite score of 36, per the same UT Austin admissions page cited above. Check your score with the [SAT score calculator](/sat-score-calculator/) or the [ACT score calculator](/act-score-calculator/).



## **How Class Rank Is Actually Used Under the Rule**

Under 19 TAC 5.5(e), the rank that matters is the rank at the end of 11th grade, the middle of 12th grade, or graduation, whichever is most recent at the application deadline, reported as a specific number out of a specific class size by your graduating or expected school. So rank can change between those snapshots. For example, rank 45 out of 500 is Top 9 percent, so it qualifies at top 10 percent schools but not at UT Austin. Rank 26 out of 500 is 5.2 percent, so it also misses UT Austin's top 5 percent cutoff. Confirm with your counselor exactly which rank your school reports and when, since that's the number that gets submitted. For how schools calculate and lock an official rank, see [what your official class rank is](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/what-is-my-class-rank/).



## **How to Check If You Qualify**

1. **Find your official class rank and class size.** Your transcript, student portal, or counselor can confirm this, covered in full in the guide linked above.
2. **Convert your rank into a percentile.** [Calculate your class rank percentile](https://studentcalctools.com/class-rank-calculator/) to see exactly where you stand, since raw rank alone doesn't tell you much without knowing your percentile.
3. **Compare your percentile against the specific school's current threshold.** Top 10 percent for most Texas public universities, top 5 percent for UT Austin as of the Summer/Fall 2026 cycle, and check the individual school's admissions page for the exact figure for your application year, since these numbers can change annually.
4. **If you're borderline, don't rely on rank alone.** A percentile close to but not quite meeting the cutoff still leaves room for a strong application through the school's regular holistic review process. Check your standing with the [GPA calculator](/gpa-calculator/) as well, since GPA is part of a holistic application.

For context on how your percentile compares more broadly, not just for Texas automatic admission but for competitiveness overall, see [what is a good class rank](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/what-is-a-good-class-rank/).

## **Common Misconceptions About the Rule**

- **It guarantees a spot at the university, not in a specific major.** Automatic admission gets you into the school. Competitive majors like computer science, business, or engineering can still have separate, more selective requirements even for automatically admitted students.
- **It only applies to Texas public universities.** Private universities and out-of-state schools aren't covered by the rule at all.
- **You must graduate from an accredited Texas high school.** Attending school out of state, even briefly, or graduating from certain non-accredited programs can affect eligibility, confirm your specific situation with your counselor.
- **The rule doesn't guarantee financial aid or any other benefit**, it's strictly an admission guarantee.
- **UT Austin's threshold isn't fixed.** Unlike the flat top 10 percent used elsewhere, UT Austin recalculates its cutoff based on demand, meaning the exact percentage can shift from year to year. Always check the current published number rather than assuming it matches a prior year.
