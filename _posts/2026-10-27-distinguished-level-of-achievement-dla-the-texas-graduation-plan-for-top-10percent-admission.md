**To qualify for Texas top 10% automatic admission, a student must also earn the Distinguished Level of Achievement (DLA) under the Foundation High School Program, or meet the ACT or SAT benchmarks set in state law. DLA means 26 credits, including Algebra II, four credits each in math and science, and an endorsement.**

Many Texas students hear that being in the top 10% of the class guarantees admission to a public university. It does not, on its own. Rank is only the first requirement, and the second is about the courses on your transcript. This guide explains what the DLA is, what it requires, how it connects to class rank, and what happens if you are in the top 10% without it. If you want to know where you stand on rank first, you can [check if you're top 10%](https://studentcalctools.com/class-rank-calculator/) with our class rank calculator. For the rule itself, see our guide to the [Texas Top 10% Rule](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/texas-top-10-percent-rule/).



## **Key takeaways**

- The DLA is a designation inside the Foundation High School Program. It is not a separate diploma.
- It requires 26 credits, Algebra II, four credits each in math and science, and an endorsement.
- Top 10% automatic admission needs rank plus either the DLA or the ACT or SAT benchmarks, according to TEA's eligibility notice.
- UT Austin uses a tighter rank line, the top 5%, in the entry terms TEA and Katy ISD list.
- Rank is measured at a specific time and reported as a number out of a total class size.



## **What is the Distinguished Level of Achievement?**

The Distinguished Level of Achievement is a higher level of the Texas Foundation High School Program. TEA's 2025 Graduation Toolkit describes it as a high level of [academic achievement earned by going beyond the Foundation High School Program](https://tea.texas.gov/about-tea/newsroom/brochures/tea-graduation-toolkit-2025.pdf). It requires 26 credits, including Algebra II, four credits each in math and science, and an endorsement.

A few useful facts:

- **It shows up on your records.** The school must show on your transcript whether you have satisfied the DLA or are on schedule to satisfy it, according to [TEA's automatic admission notice](https://tea.texas.gov/educators/graduation-information/eligibility-notification-automatic-college-admission-2028.pdf).
- **It is not the same as an endorsement.** An endorsement is a focus area. The DLA is earned on top of one.
- **There is a second way to earn it.** TEA says students who graduate through the Texas First Program pathway also earn the DLA designation.

## **DLA requirements: what the TEA list says**

TEA's toolkit lists the 22-credit Foundation program and the extra credits that turn it into an endorsement plan and a DLA. Here is the comparison:


|  |  |  |  |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Subject** | **Foundation (22 credits)** | **Foundation + endorsement (26 credits)** | **DLA (26 credits)** |
| English | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Math | 3 | 4 | 4, including Algebra II |
| Science | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| Social studies | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Languages other than English | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Physical education | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Fine arts | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Electives | 5 | 7 | 7 |
| Endorsement | No | Yes | Yes |
| Algebra II required | No | Only for the STEM endorsement | Yes |


The jump from 22 to 26 credits comes from one extra credit each in math and science, plus two more electives. TEA also says that completing an endorsement on its own does not make Algebra II mandatory, except in the STEM endorsement, so a student can reach 26 credits and still miss the DLA.

A few details that matter in practice:

- **Algebra II is not required to graduate.** A district graduation sheet notes that the state does not require it for the Foundation diploma, but students who skip it [will not be eligible for automatic college admission or certain financial aid, including the TEXAS Grant](https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/3405/Aisd/271c2356-51c0-439f-8edd-dfade9f3704e/State_Graduation_Requirements__1_.pdf?disposition=inline).
- **TEA answers the math question directly.** Its [math FAQ](https://tea.texas.gov/educators/subject-areas/mathematics-frequently-asked-questions) says the DLA needs Algebra II plus any other math courses required by the Foundation program and your chosen endorsement.
- **Your district picks the endorsements it offers.** A school that offers only one must offer Multidisciplinary Studies, according to TEA.



## **DLA vs Foundation vs endorsements**

These terms get mixed up, so here is how they fit together:


|  |  |  |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Piece** | **What it is** | **Needed for top 10% automatic admission?** |
| Foundation High School Program | The 22-credit base diploma plan | No, not by itself |
| Endorsement | A focus area such as STEM or Business and Industry, which brings the total to 26 credits | No, not by itself |
| Distinguished Level of Achievement | The designation earned with 26 credits, Algebra II and an endorsement | Yes, or the test route below |
| Performance acknowledgment | A recognition for things like dual credit, AP or IB exams, bilingualism or a certification | No |


So if a counselor asks which graduation plan lets you compete for top 10% automatic admission, the answer is the Foundation High School Program with the Distinguished Level of Achievement.



## **The ACT and SAT route**

This is the part many guides skip. TEA's graduation toolkit says students must earn the DLA for the top 10% law. TEA's eligibility notice, which follows the statute (Texas Education Code Section 51.803), lists two options. An applicant must either:

1. earn the distinguished level of achievement under the Foundation High School Program, or
2. satisfy ACT's College Readiness Benchmarks, or earn an SAT score of at least 1,500 out of 2,400 or the equivalent.

For most students, the DLA route is the practical one. If you are looking at the test route, here is what to know:

- **ACT benchmarks:** the University of North Texas [lists them as 18 in English, 22 in reading, 22 in math and 23 in science](https://www.unt.edu/admissions/freshman/admission-requirements.html).
- **SAT equivalent:** the notice states the standard on the old 2,400-point scale. UNT lists the equivalent for tests taken since March 2016 as 1090 out of 1600. Confirm the figure with your target university.
- **It does not replace the other rules.** You still need to meet the rank requirement, apply by the deadline and meet the university's curriculum requirements.

The statute also has a fairness rule. If you completed the DLA courses that were available to you but could not finish the rest because your school did not offer them at the right times, [TEA's notice says you are considered to have met the requirement](https://tea.texas.gov/educators/graduation-information/eligibility-notification-automatic-college-admission-2028.pdf). Ask your counselor to document this if it applies.\



## **How DLA connects to class rank**

The DLA is the course requirement. Rank is the performance requirement. You need both, and here is how rank works:

- **Top 10%.** A student qualifies with a GPA in the top 10% of the graduating class. The rule says the top 10% [cannot contain more than 10% of the total class size](https://tea.texas.gov/educators/graduation-information/eligibility-notification-automatic-college-admission-2028.pdf), so the cutoff rounds down. For a class of 412, that is rank 41. See our guide to the [top 10 percent cutoff](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/top-10-percent-of-class/) for the math.
- **UT Austin.** UT Austin applies a tighter line. TEA's October 2026 notice says applicants for summer or fall 2028 or spring 2029 must be in the top 5%, and [Katy ISD lists the same top 5% line](https://www.katyisd.org/academic-advising/explanation-of-automatic-college-admission) for summer or fall 2026 or spring 2027. For a class of 412, the top 5% is rank 20. Check the line for your own entry year.
- **When rank is measured.** Rank is based on the end of 11th grade, the middle of 12th grade or graduation, whichever is most recent at the application deadline.
- **How it is reported.** Your school or district reports rank as a specific number out of a specific total class size, and it decides the rank itself.

## **Top 10% without the DLA: what happens?**

If you are in the top 10% but have not met the DLA or the test route, you do not qualify for automatic admission. Texas State University says students graduating under the Foundation program [must show proof of the distinguished level of achievement](https://www.admissions.txst.edu/future-students/tuas.html) to be eligible for top 10% automatic admission. You can still apply, but you would be reviewed like any other applicant instead of being guaranteed a spot.

There are money effects too:

- **Top 10 Percent Scholarship Program.** The state's rule for the scholarship says a student must have graduated in the top 10% and [completed the DLA or an older equivalent curriculum](https://sboe.texas.gov/academics/graduation-information/explanation-of-automatic-college-admission-fig.-61.1201a1.pdf).
- **TEXAS Grant.** [Deer Park ISD, citing TEA](https://www.dpisd.org/apps/pages/index.jsp?uREC_ID=1571939&type=d&pREC_ID=1700543), says the DLA positions students among the first in line for the grant, for students who are financially qualified.



## **Automatic admission eligibility checklist**

Here is one checklist that puts rank, the DLA and the test route together. It follows TEA's notice and the statute:

1. **Check your rank.** Are you in the top 10% (or the top 5% if you are applying to UT Austin in a term where that line applies)? Remember the cutoff rounds down.
2. **Check when your rank is measured.** It is the most recent of end of 11th grade, middle of 12th grade or graduation at the application deadline.
3. **Check your course requirement.** Do you have, or are you on schedule for, the DLA? That means 26 credits, Algebra II, four math, four science and an endorsement.
4. **Or check the test route.** Do you meet the ACT College Readiness Benchmarks or the SAT standard, using your university's published equivalent?
5. **Check the transcript.** Your high school transcript or diploma must show whether you have satisfied, or are on schedule to satisfy, the DLA.
6. **Meet the university's own curriculum requirements.** Each public university can set these.
7. **Apply before the deadline.** Automatic admission is not automatic if you miss the application date.

## **How to check your DLA status**

- **Ask your counselor** to confirm whether your plan includes an endorsement, four math credits with Algebra II and four science credits.
- **Look at your transcript.** It should show whether you have met the DLA or are on schedule.
- **Use a planning tool.** TEA's toolkit points students to MapMyGrad to build a personal graduation plan, and it says the plan should be completed by the end of eighth grade.
- **Check the timing of Algebra II.** Because it is the course that most often decides the DLA, make sure it is on your schedule early enough.



## **Common mistakes to avoid**

- **Assuming rank alone is enough.** It is only half of the requirement.
- **Assuming three math credits are enough.** The DLA needs four, including Algebra II.
- **Assuming every university uses the top 10% line.** UT Austin uses a tighter one for the entry terms listed above.
- **Relying on the test route without checking the equivalent.** The notice uses the old 2,400-point SAT scale, so confirm the figure your university uses.
- **Waiting too long to plan.** A missed course can be hard to fit in later.
