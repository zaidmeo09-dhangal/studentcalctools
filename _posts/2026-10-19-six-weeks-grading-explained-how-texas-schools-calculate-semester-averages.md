**Many Texas schools split each semester into three six weeks grading periods, then combine those three averages with the semester exam to get your semester grade. A common formula counts each six weeks twice and the exam once, then divides by 7, but your district sets the exact weights.**

That last part trips up a lot of students. Your report card shows a new grade every six weeks, but the number that lands on your transcript is the semester average, and it is rarely a plain average of those grades. If you want to test your own numbers while you read, the ++[semester grade calculator](https://studentcalctools.com/blog/semester-grade-calculator/)++ has built-in formats for the 2/7 and 1/7 split, the 30/30/30/10 split and nine weeks grading periods.

This guide shows how the math works in real Texas districts, with links to the original grading documents, so you can compare your own school's rules.



## Key takeaways

- A six weeks grading period is one of about six report card periods in a school year, which means three per semester.
- Texas law makes each district adopt its own grading policy, so there is no single statewide formula.
- Fort Worth ISD counts each six weeks as 2/7 of the semester grade and the exam as 1/7. Era ISD uses 30/30/30/10.
- Major grades (tests, projects, papers) usually count more than minor grades (classwork, homework, quizzes).
- Your district's grading guidelines or student handbook always beat a general rule of thumb.



## What is a six weeks grading period?

A six weeks grading period is a block of about six weeks of school that ends with a report card grade for each class. Schools on this system usually run six periods a year, so each semester has three. Most districts also send a progress report partway through. Era ISD, for example, ++[sends a progress report after the third week and issues report cards at the end of each six-week period++.]([https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/5992/Eisd/f69b67e8-053d-412a-b288-650bb8dbd88c/26-27-Era-ISD-Grading-Guidelines.docx-%281%29-%281%29.pdf?disposition=inline](https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/5992/Eisd/f69b67e8-053d-412a-b288-650bb8dbd88c/26-27-Era-ISD-Grading-Guidelines.docx-%281%29-%281%29.pdf?disposition=inline))

The periods are not always exactly six weeks. In Fort Worth ISD's 2024-25 calendar, the first period ran from August 13 to September 20, while the third ran from November 4 to December 19. Start dates and holidays stretch or shrink each block.

Some districts use a nine weeks grading period instead. The idea is the same, but there are fewer, larger blocks:


|  |  |  |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| Grading Schedule | **Six weeks** | **Nine weeks** |
| Grading periods per year | 6 | 4 |
| Grading periods per semester | 3 | 2 |
| Weight of each period in Fort Worth ISD's formula | 2/7 each | 3/7 each |
| Semester exam in Fort Worth ISD's formula | 1/7 | 1/7 |


Texas does not set one formula for everyone. ++[Texas Education Code Chapter 28](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/ED/htm/ED.28.htm)++ (Section 28.0216) requires each school district to adopt its own grading policy before each school year. That is why a student in one district can get a different semester average than a student with the same grades in the next district.

## **What are major grades and minor grades?**

Many districts split the grades inside a six weeks period into two groups. Major grades are the big assessments, such as unit tests, performance tests, major papers and projects. Minor grades are the smaller daily items, such as classwork, homework, participation and quizzes. ++[Plano ISD's board policy](https://pisd.diligent.community/document/9b2105f5-1b2e-4de3-96fd-b43b30d5d9cb/)++ lists these examples.

Major grades usually carry more weight because they show whether you learned a whole unit. Here is how a few Texas districts weight grades inside one grading period:


|  |  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **District** | **Weighting inside a grading period** |
| ++[Era ISD](https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/5992/Eisd/f69b67e8-053d-412a-b288-650bb8dbd88c/26-27-Era-ISD-Grading-Guidelines.docx-%281%29-%281%29.pdf?disposition=inline)++ (grades 6 to 12) | 45% daily grades and 55% test grades in each six weeks |
| ++[Dickinson ISD](https://www.dickinsonisd.org/upload/page/0381/Dickinson%20ISD%20Grading%20and%20Reporting%20Procedures%202024-25.pdf)++ (2024-25) | Regular classes: 50% minor and 50% major. Honors and AP: 40% minor and 60% major. This district uses nine weeks periods. |
| ++[Plano ISD](https://pisd.diligent.community/document/9b2105f5-1b2e-4de3-96fd-b43b30d5d9cb/)++ | About three-fourths major and one-fourth minor, according to board policy |
| ++[Fort Worth ISD](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1743630242/fwisdorg/b6fql6x3q1tg00nquaqq/SecondaryGuidetoGradeReporting20242025830242.pdf)++ (2024-25) | Teachers set category weights and the principal approves them. Homework cannot count more than 10%, and no single assignment can count more than 20% of the six weeks grade. |


Why does this matter? If tests make up 55% of your six weeks grade, one bad test hurts more than a missing homework assignment. To see your current six weeks average, enter your category scores and weights into the ++[grade calculator](https://studentcalctools.com/grade-calculator/)++.

Here is a quick example with Era ISD's weights. A student has a 92 average on daily grades and an 84 average on tests:

- Daily grades: 92 × 0.45 = 41.4
- Tests: 84 × 0.55 = 46.2
- Six weeks average: 41.4 + 46.2 = 87.6



## **How do you calculate a semester average from six weeks grades?**

Once the three six weeks averages are final, your district's formula combines them with the semester exam. These formulas come straight from district documents:


|  |  |  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **District** | **Formula** | **Semester exam weight** |
| ++[Fort Worth ISD](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1743630242/fwisdorg/b6fql6x3q1tg00nquaqq/SecondaryGuidetoGradeReporting20242025830242.pdf)++, six weeks | 2/7 + 2/7 + 2/7 + 1/7 | 1/7 (about 14.3%) |
| ++[Fort Worth ISD](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1743630242/fwisdorg/b6fql6x3q1tg00nquaqq/SecondaryGuidetoGradeReporting20242025830242.pdf)++, nine weeks | 3/7 + 3/7 + 1/7 | 1/7 (about 14.3%) |
| ++[Era ISD](https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/5992/Eisd/f69b67e8-053d-412a-b288-650bb8dbd88c/26-27-Era-ISD-Grading-Guidelines.docx-%281%29-%281%29.pdf?disposition=inline)++, six weeks | 30% + 30% + 30% + 10% | 10% |
| ++Garland ISD++, nine-week cycles | Average the two cycles, with the exam counted on top | 1/7 of the semester average |


Plano ISD's board policy goes further and counts the exam as 20% of the semester grade in grades 9 to 12 and 10% in grades 7 and 8. So the exam is not worth the same everywhere.

The Fort Worth ISD version is the one most students run into, so here is the shortcut for it:

**Semester average = (2 × first six weeks + 2 × second six weeks + 2 × third six weeks + exam) ÷ 7**

If a class has no semester exam, the periods are simply averaged. Garland ISD's regulation says exactly that for classes without an exam.

## **Worked example: one semester, two districts**

Say a student earns 88, 82 and 91 in the three six weeks, then scores 76 on the semester exam.

**Fort Worth ISD method (2/7 each, exam 1/7):**


|  |  |  |
| ------------- | -------- | ---------- |
| **Item** | **Math** | **Result** |
| 1st six weeks | 88 × 2 | 176 |
| 2nd six weeks | 82 × 2 | 164 |
| 3rd six weeks | 91 × 2 | 182 |
| Semester exam | 76 × 1 | 76 |
| Total |  | 598 |


598 ÷ 7 = 85.43. Fort Worth ISD rounds a decimal of .5 or higher up to the next whole number, so 85.43 posts as an 85.

**Era ISD method (30/30/30/10):**

- 88 × 0.30 = 26.4
- 82 × 0.30 = 24.6
- 91 × 0.30 = 27.3
- 76 × 0.10 = 7.6
- Total: 85.9

Same grades, 85.4 in one district and 85.9 in the other. That gap is small, but it can decide a letter grade when you sit near a cutoff.

### **How much can the semester exam change your grade?**

In the 2/7 and 1/7 formula, every point on the exam moves your semester average by about 0.14 points. Using the same six weeks grades:


|  |  |
| -------------- | -------------------- |
| **Exam score** | **Semester average** |
| 50 | 81.7 |
| 76 | 85.4 |
| 100 | 88.9 |


A 50-point swing on the exam moves the semester average by about seven points. That matters, but it will not erase three solid six weeks.

### **What exam score do you need for a target semester grade?**

Rearrange the formula and you get this:

**Exam score needed = 7 × target average − 2 × (sum of your three six weeks grades)**

With 88, 82 and 91, the sum is 261, and 2 × 261 = 522:


|  |  |
| --------------------------- | --------------------- |
| **Target semester average** | **Exam score needed** |
| 85 | 73 |
| 88 | 94 |
| 90 | 108 (not possible) |


A result over 100 means the target is out of reach with that exam alone. Rounding can help a little. Fort Worth ISD rounds .5 up, so an 85 needs an average of 84.5 or higher, which works out to an exam score of about 70 in this example.



## **Do semester exam exemptions change the math?**

They can. Some districts let students skip the exam if they meet certain rules. In Fort Worth ISD, seniors can skip the spring exam if they hold an 85 or higher in the course and have no more than three absences. School-related absences do not count toward that limit.

When an exam is skipped, your district's policy decides how the semester average is built, so do not assume the six weeks simply get averaged. Check the guidelines or ask your counselor.



## **Why do six weeks grades matter beyond the report card?**

- **Sports and activities.** Under Texas No Pass No Play rules, as ++[Era ISD describes them](https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/5992/Eisd/f69b67e8-053d-412a-b288-650bb8dbd88c/26-27-Era-ISD-Grading-Guidelines.docx-%281%29-%281%29.pdf?disposition=inline)++, a grade below 70 at the end of a grading period in an academic class means at least three school weeks away from extracurricular activities. AP, IB, honors and dual credit courses in core subjects are exempt.
- **Course credit.** Districts like Fort Worth ISD and Era ISD require a 70 for credit, and a student who fails one semester can still earn full credit if the average of both semesters is at least 70. Era ISD also cites the state's 90% attendance requirement for credit.

## **How can you find your school's exact formula?**

1. Search your district name plus "grading guidelines." Many Texas districts publish a guide, and board policy code EIA covers grading.
2. Read your student handbook and the course syllabus your teacher handed out in the first week.
3. Check your parent or student portal. It often shows each six weeks average.
4. Ask your teacher or counselor three things: how much each six weeks counts, how much the exam counts, and how averages are rounded.

## **Common mistakes to avoid**

- Averaging the three six weeks equally when your district also counts an exam.
- Using nine weeks weights (3/7) on a six weeks schedule (2/7).
- Rounding each grade before combining them. Keep the decimals until the last step.
- Treating a calculator result as official. Your report card and portal always win.
